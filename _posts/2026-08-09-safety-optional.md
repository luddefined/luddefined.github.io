This game is about forklifts. It was the main assignment in our third and final year of university (excluding our dissertations). Our group was named *Runtime Error*, and was composed of ten people, including me (though depending on which other eight people you ask, they may say that number is one-too high). I was mostly responsible for implementing anything that involved a crate, as well as some of the VFX systems, in addition to offering other minor help wherever was needed. I also made that trailer you definitely had just watched before reading this, which the powers of hindsight and reflection has told me that it might have been a bit too long...

It is the largest project I've ever worked on so far. Definitely an eye-opening experience on major fronts; project management and game design taking the spotlight of "most insight gathered". Many times did our project manager - though I am very grateful for the work they put in and definitely understanding of the amount of stress it must have been - seem to proclaim about "horses led to water yet withholding the desire to drink", which has been a learning lesson for all of us in understanding how to implement and execute a clear, concise plan for development. 

Despite all of the trouble we went through, it's still a game that I'm quite proud to have made, although it does have quite an infamous reputation between my friend group. This is where that second insight came from, as it is a game that is *just* about forklifts, not so much about the party aspect. In retrospect, there were so many other ways to take our game and other things we could have implemented:
- Less 'trying to make the game about funny moments' - very early playtesting with others had all players look like they were giggling when they were just messing about, which I think our game unfortunately missed out on capitalising.
- Less realistic - I see a world where this game is way more 'cartoony', possibly with a more 'idol-like' visual design phiolosophy, with an abundance of joy and whimsy.

Despite all of this, I'm still happy with what we were able to make. If anything, I'm way more eager to make something that surpasses this than be upset over what this could have been like.
